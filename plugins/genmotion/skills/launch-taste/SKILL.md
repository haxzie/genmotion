# Launch taste: choose what the film is, so it feels intentional

A median launch film fades up a headline, pastes the landing page underneath, puts a glow in the corner and ends on a logo. A premium one says one sentence, makes it visible with the product's own material, holds back everywhere except at one moment, and lets sound arrive exactly where the picture changes. The difference is a handful of choices made before any scene is built. This skill is those choices.

It sits between `direction` (the process: proposition, idea, family, curve, beat table) and the owner skill (the format's structure: `launch-playbook`, `announce-feature`, `brand-sting`, `announce-milestone`, `app-store-preview`, `freeform-video`). Use it to answer direction's Step 3 (the idea) and Step 4 (the style family) for any launch-type film, and to judge the cut at the end. `motion-language` and `sound-design` still own every number for moves and mix; this skill only says which of them to reach for and why. Frames are at 30 fps.

> **These are references, not rules.** The numbers, beat sheets, style families and examples here are starting points distilled from work that landed well, not a recipe to fill in. The user's instructions always win over anything in this skill. When a different idea serves the brief better, propose it and try it: change the structure, break a default, invent a device, and write what you changed and why in `VIDEO.md`. What stays fixed is correctness: determinism, legibility and safe zones, loudness and clipping, licences and credits, and never putting words in a real person's mouth. The checks at the end are a quality bar to clear, not a template to reproduce.

Where these principles come from: a study of twenty motion-design launch, feature, milestone and brand films (design-engineer launches, open-source tools and showcase pieces; no live action), graded strictly, plus GenMotion's own templates. Moves were learned only from the films that worked; the weak ones supplied the anti-patterns. The set was small, so the references mark which rules several films agree on and which rest on one precise example.

## When to use

- Any launch-type film, alongside its owner: a product or app launch, a feature announcement, a brand sting, a milestone, a store preview, a freeform promo for a product or company.
- "Make this launch video feel premium", "what style should our launch film be", "our launch video looks generic", "it has no idea", "the sound feels pasted on".
- Before writing direction's Idea and Style family lines, and again at the self-critique.

Not for: edited footage of people (`video-editing`), UGC ad formats (the `ugc-*` owners), or a teaching explainer (`explainer`, though §Sound to picture still applies). It never replaces the owner: the owner's beat sheet, lengths and checks win for its format.

## Seven beliefs

1. **One sentence, made visible.** If a stranger cannot say it back after one muted view, the film is a list.
2. **The film must need time.** Ask what a still image would lose. A continuity, a convergence, a count becoming a quantity, a sound arriving on a pixel: good. "Nothing": the concept is missing, and polish will not fix it.
3. **The material is the product's own**: its nouns, units, glyphs, output and UI. Decoration (glows, blobs, particles, corner metadata) is what a film uses when it has no idea.
4. **Restraint only reads against contrast.** Stillness is confidence when one thing is clearly alive; stillness everywhere is absence.
5. **Shape beats speed.** A quiet start, a build, one peak at the biggest contrast, a calm resolve.
6. **Sound is placed by meaning**: on the event, as long as the event, rising for "yes", gone for the payoff.
7. **Direction is subtraction.** Most fixes are removals.

## The decision path

Make these in order; each narrows the next. Write the answers into `VIDEO.md` (see "Write it down").

### 1. The one thing: feel, or understand?

From the proposition, pick which the viewer leaves with. **Understand** ("it turns a URL into a spec") points to proof: the product working, a visible before and after. **Feel** ("your work follows you", "this is serious engineering", "you did a lot this year") points to a small story, an object, or a world. Both is a list; pick the lead and let the other ride along.

Then write the still-image test in one line: "As a still, this would lose ___." If the blank is empty, go to step 2 before anything else.

### 2. The concept device

Pick the device before the look. These patterns recur in films that work; `references/concept-devices.md` has each with when to use it, fresh examples, how to build it and how it goes wrong.

| Device | Reach for it when |
| --- | --- |
| The product makes the film | It produces visuals, type, layout or motion; proof beats claims |
| The invisible made felt | The benefit is "nothing went wrong" (sync, offline, backup, privacy): a small story with an interruption makes the viewer need it |
| One fixed sentence, swapped notation | One promise to several audiences |
| Many → one | Consolidation, integration, "one place" |
| Output as material | The product generates content: build the mark or the hero object from it |
| The unit becomes the quantity | A milestone or a scale claim |
| The journey | Data over time, a recap, a route |
| The edit of old → new | A rename, a replacement, a companion format |
| The timeline drives the input | The product is itself motion or a parameter |
| Two worlds, one crossing | Positioning against an incumbent idea |
| The brand becomes the UI | Logo and product must arrive in one breath |
| Change the medium, not the subject | The honest subject is a cliché (a globe, a galaxy, a network) |
| Fixed silhouette, changing skin | Versatility |
| Ranked countdown | Several results of different weight |

Sketch three (from the product's own visual world, from the feeling, from an unexpected format) and keep the least obvious one that passes direction's swap test. A device that needs the voiceover to explain it is the wrong device.

### 3. The style family

Choose the look that best carries the device, not the one that looks most "premium" in the abstract. The taste family says *why*; direction's house family letter supplies the numbers (palette, type, timings). `references/style-map.md` has each family's motion and sound personality and its classic failure; read it before writing the Palette, Type and Motion lines.

| Taste family | Says | Suits | Fails for | House family |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kinetic editorial type | A point of view, wit | Manifestos, renames, multi-audience, teasers, a film's first 3–4 s | Anything that must be seen working; type alone past ~20 s | A, or B's type on a calm ground |
| UI-led story film | "It fits your day" | Consumer and productivity software, invisible features | Infra without UI, hype | B (D for one world) |
| Maker's proof film | "It's real, run it" | Dev tools, libraries, generators, the product making its own film | Consumer emotion, products that can't show output | B |
| Product-as-instrument | Precision, depth | Models, APIs, infra, data, security | Warmth, "it's easy", visual tools | G (HUD) |
| 3D object hero | Tangible value, craft | Consumer, fintech, devices, stings, generative output on an object | Workflow products, more than one idea | C |
| Data story / journey | Achievement, earned scale | Recaps, milestones, onboarding, "everything we shipped" | One feature; under ~15 s | J, or D |
| Playful collage | Curiosity, versatility | Creative tools, identity reveals, social loops | Precise or technical brands | G (paper) |
| Dark cinematic minimal | Drama, focus | Model launches, idents, hero loops behind type | Bright feeds, friendly consumer tone | A ground, not a family: keep A, C or G's numbers on it |
| Terminal / retro | Builder credibility | CLIs, open source where the command is the CTA | Mainstream audiences | G (HUD) or A |
| Component sizzle | "Parts that behave" | UI kits, SDKs | Outcome products | B on dark, or A |

Defaults when nothing else decides: an app with screens → UI-led story or maker's proof; no UI at all → instrument (serious) or kinetic type (opinionated); one number → data or 3D object; only a logo → 3D object or collage. The owner's own default family still wins ties.

### 4. Motion personality

Set one physics from the feeling, then reuse it everywhere (`motion-language` holds the numbers):

- **Calm trust** (finance, health, enterprise, luxury): no overshoot, long ease-outs, longer holds, locked or slow camera.
- **Confident premium** (most launches): snappy in, slow settle, no bounce; a thrown object caught softly; the camera eased at both ends.
- **Playful** (creator, consumer, community): overshoot 6–10% on one role only (a button, a sticker, a stamp).
- **Technical** (instrument, terminal): stepped and digital moves plus exactly one organic counter-motion (a wave, a breath), so the world feels alive.

Then decide **what is allowed to move**: one focal element per moment, everything else settled. And decide **the rhythm**: busy passages cut fast (0.5–0.9 s), resolutions hold long (4–5 s); give the hero beat about twice the time and minor beats half. Ten beats of the same length read as a slideshow.

### 5. Sound role

Name what leads (`sound-design` decides levels and sources):

- **Music leads**: a hype film, a sting, a recap. Lock arrivals to the grid, then **cut or thin the music on the payoff** and let a tail carry the hold.
- **The product's own sound leads** (a voice tool, an audio app, anything that clicks or plays): the bed leaves when the product speaks, and stays out.
- **Voice leads**: cut in the speaker's breaths, keep a bed under it, never let a pause become a blank picture.
- **Picture leads** (muted placements): every claim lives in the frames; visualise sounds as echoes of their source's shape; sound is a reward for unmuting.

`references/sound-picture.md` has the sync rules (latency, length, pitch direction, removing the bed, anchoring the lift, tails).

### 6. The restraint budget

Write these four lines; they are what makes the film look decided:

1. **One accent**, and what it means (the moving part, the active state, the payoff word, the CTA). Nothing else gets it.
2. **One rule broken, at the climax**: the one physical shot in a flat film, the one serif word in a sans film, the one time the whole ground takes a hue, the one brightest frame. Spend it once.
3. **One transition grammar**: a signature carried by something in the frame, a workhorse, hard cuts only on the music (direction Step 7).
4. **One silence**: where sound drops away on purpose (before the reveal, under the payoff, after the hero number).

### 7. Peak placement

Put the single peak where the film's kind says, and build it from the device, never from a transition:

| Kind of film | Peak | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reveal-led launch, milestone | 25–35% | The reveal early; everything after is evidence and resolve |
| Feature story with a turn | 65–75% | A story needs its setup before the turn |
| The product's sound or output is the payoff | 85–95% | The picture calms while the product takes over |
| Sting | entrance in the first 10%, lock at 40–60% | Grab the first second, land the mark, decay |
| Hype / instrument teaser | 70–100%, with the picture simplifying as the sound climbs | Contrast between dense sound and calm picture makes the lock inevitable |

Before the peak, a calmer breath (direction's 10–30f). After it, slow down so the result has weight. Repeats that build toward it should tighten (for example swaps of 6, then 4, then 2 frames) with slight irregularity; identical spacing reads as a timer.

## Write it down

Add these lines under direction's block in `VIDEO.md`, after `Idea:` and `Style family:`:

```markdown
Still-image test: as a still, this would lose <…>
Lead: <feel|understand> — <the one thing>
Device: <pattern> — <this film's version in one line>
Style family: <house letter + name> (taste: <taste family>) — because <device / audience reason>
Motion personality: <calm trust|confident premium|playful|technical>; moves: <what may move>
Restraint: accent = <meaning> · broken rule = <what, at frame> · silence = <where>
Sound lead: <music|product|voice|picture>; payoff treatment: <cut music|bed out|hit + tail>
```

## Building it

The choices are engine-neutral. On **Three.js** (the default) the device usually becomes one persisting object or plane (`three-assets` for UI and textures, `three-camera` for the one big move, `three-transitions` for a carrier), and the restraint budget is easiest to keep with one material and one light (`three-look`). On **HyperFrames** and **React**, the same lines map to sub-compositions or scenes; keep the carrier element shared between them so a handoff is a real continuity, not two similar shapes.

## Good and bad

- Bad: "Introducing" on a dark gradient, a feature-tag cloud, five feature cards, a logo. Good: the product's own output assembles into its mark, which then opens into the first screen.
- Bad: a sync feature explained with devices and arrows. Good: a small interruption makes the viewer need sync, and the same work is simply there on the second screen.
- Bad: every component demo two seconds long under a steady track. Good: the hero component gets twice the time, the minor ones a quick montage, and the music lifts on the hero's reveal.
- Bad: a sound feature announced in silence, or under music. Good: the bed leaves on the first press, and each press is heard alone.
- Bad: a dark, moody reveal with one second of black at the head. Good: something already moving and bright at frame 0, darkness used around it.

## The taste test

Ask of the cut, on captured frames; any "no" is a note. The full anti-pattern list (what weak launch films do) is in `references/anti-patterns.md`; read it when a film "looks templated" and you cannot say why.

1. Can I say the one sentence after one muted view?
2. Would it lose its meaning as a still?
3. Is the device made of this product's own material, and would it break with a competitor's name on it?
4. Is frame 0 in motion, with the tension on screen by 1 s?
5. One peak, built from the idea, with a calmer breath before it?
6. One accent used only for meaning, one broken rule at the climax, one transition grammar?
7. Does rhythm follow the story (busy fast, resolve slow, the hero beat longer)?
8. Is each sound on or just after its event, as long as its motion, pointing the right way?
9. Does the bed step aside where the product or the payoff must be heard?
10. Does it end by arriving: a button, a tail, a held lockup that is alive?
11. Is there an element I would delete if I had to cut one? Delete it.

## Checks before you finish

1. `VIDEO.md` has the seven lines from "Write it down", and the device line names this product's own noun, unit, glyph or output.
2. `capture-frames` at frame 0 and frame 15: something is moving and the frame is not black or empty; on a dark ground the focal point is bright (not a near-black frame on a phone).
3. The peak frame named in the Direction block is inside the band for this kind of film (step 7), it shows the device, and a capture 15–30f before it is visibly calmer.
4. The accent appears only on what the Restraint line says it means (check three frames from different beats).
5. The broken rule happens once: a contact sheet (`direction`'s critique §1) shows it in one beat only.
6. Beat lengths are not uniform (films with four or more content beats): the hero beat is the longest content beat, and no four consecutive beats are all within 10% of each other in length.
7. Where the product's sound or the payoff leads, the bed's 0.5 s RMS there sits at least 10 dB below its level either side (`ffmpeg` `astats` on the music stem), and every UI cue there starts on or up to 2 frames after its first changed frame, never before (the sound being sold is exposed, so it must read as caused).
8. The last 1.5 s has a tail or a button (the music is not at full level on the last frame), and the end card holds with one ambient behaviour.
9. Nothing from `references/anti-patterns.md` §Template tells is on screen.
10. The owner's checks and direction's self-critique pass.
