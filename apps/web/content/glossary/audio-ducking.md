**Audio ducking** automatically lowers a background track's volume — typically music — whenever a foreground track like narration is present, then restores it once the speech ends. It's what keeps a voiceover intelligible over a music bed without silencing the music outright.

Ducking is usually driven by the presence of speech on another track rather than manual keyframing, so the mix adapts automatically as a script changes.

See also: [Loudness Normalization (LUFS)](/glossary/loudness-normalization), [Waveform](/glossary/waveform).
